# Rhino Brand Voice and Copy Rules

**PBI:** B-021  
**Status:** Approved foundation  
**Owner:** Brand, Content, Product, Technical, Business, and Legal  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-07-31

## Core principle

Rhino explains technical equipment clearly enough for a new buyer and
precisely enough for an experienced lapidary.

The voice is technical first and human when a customer needs to make a decision,
prepare for delivery, solve a problem, or request support.

## Voice attributes

### Direct

State what the product does, what the customer needs, and what happens next.

### Precise

Use verified terms, dimensions, compatibility, and operational facts.

### Calm

Avoid artificial urgency, exaggerated enthusiasm, and unsupported superiority.

### Practical

Explain setup, maintenance, delivery, included components, replacement parts,
and workshop requirements.

### Supportive

Help a customer recover from uncertainty without speaking down to beginners or
frustrating experienced users.

### Transparent

State exclusions, unknowns, freight requirements, regional limitations, and
unconfirmed compatibility plainly.

## Narrative perspective

Use a hybrid system.

### Neutral technical voice

Use neutral language for:

- Specifications
- Compatibility
- Included equipment
- Maintenance
- Dimensions
- Electrical requirements
- Product classifications
- Warnings
- Policies

Example:

`This wheel has a 6 in diameter and is intended for the listed compatible machines.`

### First-person plural

Use `we` only when Rhino or the responsible business actually performs the
action.

Examples:

- `We will review your support request.`
- `We can help confirm compatibility before you order.`

Do not use `we` when the responsible entity is unresolved.

### Second person

Use `you` for instructions and customer decisions.

Examples:

- `Confirm the required voltage before ordering.`
- `Keep the packaging if the shipment arrives damaged.`

## Writing rules

1. Lead with the customer decision.
2. Put the most important limitation near the claim it qualifies.
3. Explain technical terms at first use.
4. Use approved product naming and units.
5. Distinguish included, required, optional, recommended, and compatible.
6. Use active voice.
7. Keep sentences moderate in length.
8. Use lists for procedures, included items, and comparisons.
9. Use paragraphs for explanation and context.
10. Use tables for genuinely comparable technical data.
11. State uncertainty rather than filling a gap.
12. Link to full policies instead of compressing every exclusion into a badge.
13. Name the destination of a link or CTA.
14. Keep legal, warranty, certification, and patent claims tied to evidence.
15. Do not reproduce source-document errors merely because they appear in an
    old brochure or policy.

## Punctuation and capitalization

- Use sentence case for headings, buttons, labels, and badges.
- Preserve official machine names such as `EM-1`.
- Avoid repeated exclamation marks.
- Avoid all-capital paragraphs.
- Use a colon to introduce a product type after a model when approved.
- Use the terminology guide for units and measurements.
- Use the serial comma when it improves technical clarity.

## Claim discipline

### Permitted without special review

- Product role
- Approved product title
- Confirmed compatibility
- Current Shopify availability
- Approved dimensions and specifications
- Plain navigation and support instructions

### Requires technical review

- Motor power
- Speed
- Voltage
- Capacity
- Dimensions and weight
- Supported blade or wheel sizes
- Performance
- Included components
- Compatibility
- Maintenance intervals

### Requires business or legal review

- Warranty
- Manufacturer identity
- Supplier identity
- Exclusive territory
- Patent or patent-pending status
- Certification
- Durability or safety claims
- Refund or return promises
- Freight and international responsibility
- Response-time promises

## Prohibited patterns

Avoid:

- Best
- Ultimate
- Indestructible
- Professional grade
- Built to last, unless supported by approved evidence
- Industry leading
- Revolutionary
- Game changing
- Universal
- Guaranteed compatibility
- Ships anywhere
- Maintenance free
- Buy with confidence
- Hurry
- Limited time, unless an actual maintained promotion exists

## Channel rules

### Homepage

Lead with:

- What Rhino equipment helps customers do
- How to choose the right equipment
- The relationship among machines, parts, consumables, and support

Avoid generic corporate or wholesale language.

### Product cards

Use:

- Complete product name
- Product role
- Owning machine family
- Price or approved purchase state
- One critical availability or fulfillment signal

Do not place promotional paragraphs inside cards.

### Machine product pages

Order information around customer decisions:

1. What the machine does
2. Who it suits
3. Purchase and availability
4. Required workshop conditions
5. Specifications
6. Included equipment
7. Compatible products
8. Freight and delivery
9. Warranty and support
10. Manuals and maintenance

### Parts and consumables

Lead with:

- What the item is
- Which machines it fits
- Size, grit, arbor, or other differentiator
- Quantity or package contents
- Replacement or installation guidance

### Support

Use calm, explicit recovery language.

State:

- What information the customer should provide
- What to keep
- Who receives the request
- What action the customer should take next

### Freight

Do not hide freight information in policy text.

State:

- Whether freight may be required
- Whether the displayed amount is an estimate
- Whether delivery timing requires confirmation
- What the customer should do before ordering when uncertain

### Warranty

Use product-specific summaries.

Do not apply a universal one-year statement when the policy contains 90-day
exceptions or product-specific exclusions.

### International

State:

- Whether availability requires confirmation
- Voltage or regional configuration requirements
- Customer responsibility for brokerage or customs when approved
- Whether service and warranty coverage differ

Do not imply universal sale, voltage, certification, or service coverage.

## Before and after examples

### Homepage hero

Before:

`Grow your business with us`

After:

`Choose lapidary equipment for the work you need to do`

### Generic promotional heading

Before:

`Proven bestsellers`

After:

`Machines and workshop essentials`

Use `Best sellers` only when a governed sales rule exists.

### Product relationship

Before:

`Other industry favorites`

After, when compatibility is confirmed:

`Compatible parts and accessories`

After, when compatibility is not structured:

`Related products`

### Product tab

Before:

`Product features`

After:

`Capabilities and included equipment`

### Care tab

Before:

`Materials and care`

After:

`Maintenance and care`

### Generic merchandising tab

Before:

`Merchandising tips`

After:

Remove it, or replace it with `Setup and operation` only when approved content
exists.

### EM-1 title and subtitle

Title:

`Rhino EM-1: Multi-Operation Lapidary Machine`

Subtitle:

`The Everything Machine`

The subtitle is positioning copy, not the canonical product title.

### Compatibility

Avoid:

`Works with most Rhino machines`

Use:

`Fits Rhino EM-1`

only when the structured relation is confirmed.

## Copy ownership

| Copy type | Source |
|---|---|
| Reusable UI labels | Locale files |
| Product titles and descriptions | Shopify product data |
| Technical specifications | Structured product data |
| Compatibility | Structured product relations |
| Machine family summaries | Metaobjects or governed pages |
| Homepage and editorial content | Theme sections or Shopify pages |
| Policies | Shopify policy administration |
| Warranty summaries | Product-specific governed content |
| Form success and error messages | Locale files or form implementation |
| Notifications | Shopify notification templates |
| Legal claims | Claims register and approved source |

## Review checklist

- Does the copy help a customer decide or act?
- Is every technical claim sourced?
- Is every legal or operational promise approved?
- Are included and optional items distinct?
- Is compatibility explicit?
- Is the responsible organization clear?
- Is the wording understandable on mobile?
- Does the copy contain a generic Trade placeholder?
- Does the CTA name its result?
- Does the copy avoid exaggerated language?
