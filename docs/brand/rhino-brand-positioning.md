# Rhino Brand Positioning and Identity Architecture

**PBIs:** B-002 and B-003  
**Status:** Proposed; not approved for storefront use  
**Evidence basis:** Epic B discovery dated 2026-07-30  
**Required reviewers:** Brand Owner, Business Owner, Legal or Claims Reviewer, Product Owner

## 1. Positioning foundation

### Proposed brand role

Rhino Lapidary is a specialized lapidary equipment brand and storefront centered on understandable machinery, practical workshop capability, compatible accessories and parts, and support throughout ownership.

This wording is intentionally provisional. It avoids asserting a legal manufacturer, seller, or warranty role until the identity architecture is approved.

### Proposed primary promise

**Build a capable lapidary workshop around equipment you can understand, use, maintain, and support over time.**

### Supporting promises

1. **Technical clarity:** Explain what each machine does, what it includes, what it requires, and which parts or consumables fit it.
2. **Workshop practicality:** Present real setup, operation, maintenance, water management, space, power, and delivery considerations.
3. **Ecosystem confidence:** Help owners find compatible accessories, replacement parts, consumables, manuals, and support.
4. **Purchase reassurance:** Make freight, lead time, warranty, service, returns, and support responsibilities visible before purchase.
5. **Craft respect:** Connect machinery to the lapidary process without obscuring specifications behind decorative language.

## 2. Audience and purchase-journey messaging

| Audience | Primary question | Lead message | Required proof and reassurance | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New lapidary buyer | What equipment do I actually need? | Start with the operations, materials, space, power, and budget required for the work you want to do. | Plain-language machine roles, included items, setup needs, comparison guidance, support contact | Assuming product-name knowledge or using unexplained technical terms |
| Experienced lapidary | How does this compare with equipment I know? | Evaluate capability, specifications, compatibility, maintenance, and workflow without marketing detours. | Complete specifications, dimensions, power, wheel or blade details, water system, parts availability | Vague “premium” claims or hidden specifications |
| Existing Rhino owner | Which part or consumable fits my machine? | Find compatible parts and consumables by machine family, specification, and task. | Compatibility labels, SKUs, dimensions, manuals, replacement intervals | Requiring customers to infer compatibility from titles |
| Professional workshop | Is this reliable and practical for sustained use? | Show operational requirements, maintenance expectations, throughput-relevant specifications, service, and parts support. | Technical documentation, support process, freight, warranty, lead time | Unsupported durability or productivity claims |
| Wholesale, dealer, school, club, or institution | Can we purchase, support, and account for this equipment appropriately? | Provide clear contact, tax, quote, purchase-order, delivery, documentation, and support paths. | Responsible entity, pricing or quote rules, freight, documentation, account support | Blurring retail and institutional purchase rules |
| International visitor | Can this machine be purchased and used in my region? | Confirm voltage, plug, certification, shipping availability, duties, service, and warranty scope before purchase. | Market availability, electrical specifications, territorial support, certification evidence | Implying universal availability or compatibility |
| Mobile visitor from video, QR code, trade show, or social link | Is this the correct product and what should I do next? | Give a fast identity, capability, compatibility, price or inquiry path, and support route. | Concise summary, clear media, dominant action, specs at a glance | Large decorative introductions that delay product understanding |

## 3. Brand voice

### Voice traits

- Direct
- Technically precise
- Practical
- Calm
- Supportive
- Respectful of both beginners and experienced lapidaries

### Writing rules

- Lead with customer decisions, not company adjectives.
- Explain technical terms at first use.
- Prefer measurable specifications and operational facts to broad quality claims.
- State uncertainty, lead time, freight, compatibility, and exclusions plainly.
- Use active voice and specific actions.
- Distinguish “included,” “required,” “compatible,” “optional,” and “recommended.”
- Never imply legal, certification, warranty, manufacturing, or exclusivity facts without an approved source.

### Tone boundary between Rhino and The Gem Shop

**Rhino voice** should explain the equipment system, technical decisions, compatibility, operation, maintenance, and ownership experience.

**The Gem Shop voice** may explain its approved sales, service, fulfillment, showroom, or supplier role where relevant.

The storefront must not merge the two voices so thoroughly that customers cannot identify who is responsible for the transaction, support, warranty, or fulfillment.

### Avoid

- “Best,” “ultimate,” “indestructible,” “professional grade,” or similar unsupported superlatives.
- Decorative geological metaphors in specifications, warnings, or purchase instructions.
- Competitor comparisons without a maintained evidence standard.
- “Made by,” “manufactured by,” “exclusive,” “certified,” “patented,” or warranty statements without current approval.
- Generic Trade language such as “industry insiders” when it does not help a lapidary customer decide.
